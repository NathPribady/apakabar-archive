# News against the grain: twelve years of Indonesia on apakabar

<p class="lede">From 1990 to 2002, John MacDougall's apakabar carried Indonesia's news, rumours and arguments past the New Order's censors. {{total}} of its messages survive. This is what they show.</p>

<div class="byline">By Nathanael Pribady · every figure computed from the archive</div>

{{stats}}

## What survives

Apakabar began on 7 October 1990 as a conference on the IGC network, run by John A. MacDougall of Indonesia Publications in Maryland. It grew into a family of moderated lists that he edited, mostly alone, until February 2002. Its database, hosted by Hamline University and later Ohio University, is gone. Only the Internet Archive's captures remain.

The captures hold {{basis}} messages. By its own folder listings, the database held {{est}}, so {{coverage_share}} survive. The list archives at indopubs.com add {{list_messages}} more. Without {{duplicates}} duplicates, {{canonical}} distinct texts remain. {{coverage_months_sentence}}

## From a foreign conference to an Indonesian forum

In 1991, {{english_1991}} of the messages were in English, written by scholars, solidarity groups and journalists abroad. Indonesian rose to {{indonesian_1994}} in 1994 and {{indonesian_1996}} in 1996, and stayed above half from then on. As commercial internet reached Indonesia, MacDougall's English summaries gave way to Indonesian articles reposted in full and to Indonesians arguing with each other: a public sphere the regime could not easily see.

## Reading the banned press by e-mail

After *Tempo*, *Editor* and *DeTIK* were banned on 21 June 1994, more of the list's news came from outlets the regime had closed or never licensed: *Tempo Interaktif*, SiaR, Xpos, *Kabar dari PIJAR* and PRD bulletins. Their share of reposted news averaged {{underground_1997_avg}} a quarter in 1997 and peaked at {{underground_peak}} in {{underground_peak_quarter}}, a thin quarter of {{underground_peak_news}} news messages, mostly SiaR bulletins.

{{figure:underground}}

Once Soeharto fell and the Habibie government stopped licensing the press, the underground share collapsed. Foreign reporting (dashed) was the main source until 1995, then about a tenth of the news, rising only when the world looked at Indonesia, as over East Timor in 1999 and after 11 September 2001.

## How fast the news travelled

The Santa Cruz massacre in Dili on 12 November 1991 reached the list {{lag_santa_cruz}}, through TAPOL. The storming of the PDI headquarters on 27 July 1996 was reported {{lag_27_july}} and drew {{after30_27_july}} messages in thirty days; the East Timor referendum of 30 August 1999 drew {{after30_referendum}}. The labour activist Marsinah, found murdered on 8 May 1993, was first mentioned {{lag_marsinah}}. That delay is the list's own: from February to July 1993 the database received messages on only {{days_1993_with_messages}} of {{days_1993}} days, and {{marsinah_survival}}.

## Two case studies

### The May 1998 sexual violence

From 13 May 1998, {{may_paragraph}} messages speak of rape or sexual violence in the same paragraph as the riots, Chinese Indonesians or the inquiries; {{may_focused}} are mainly about it. They record how the reports spread, who doubted them and how the denial was argued. The busiest month was {{may_busiest_month}}, with {{may_busiest_n}}. [Read the case study →](/analysis/may-1998)

### Gerwani and the memory of 1965

Gerwani is named in {{gerwani_total}} messages. The list carried the New Order's version of 1965 and the first open challenges to it, from former political prisoners, exiles such as Sobron Aidit, and readers of Pramoedya. [Read the case study →](/analysis/1965)
